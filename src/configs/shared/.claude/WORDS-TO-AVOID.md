# Words And Phrases To Avoid

This list applies to everything you write for me: chat answers, commit messages,
documentation, code comments, and PR descriptions. The `write-like-me` skill has a second
list for text that goes out under my name.

Replace the word. Do not keep it and add an apology for it.

## Metaphors and idioms

These have no literal meaning. A reader who is not a native speaker cannot decode them,
and they always replace a fact that you could state directly.

- `load-bearing` — Say what breaks if you remove it.
- `smoking gun` — Say what the evidence is.
- `low-hanging fruit` — Say which items are quick.
- `moving parts` — Say which components are involved.
- `rabbit hole`, `in the weeds`, `down the stack`
- `battle-tested`, `bulletproof`, `rock-solid`
- `the last mile`, `heavy lifting`, `boils down to`

## Marketing words

- `delve`, `leverage` (use `use`), `robust`, `seamless`, `streamline`, `elevate`,
  `unlock`, `powerful`, `rich`, `comprehensive`, `cutting-edge`
- `ensure` (use `make sure` or `verify`), `utilize` (use `use`), `facilitate`

## Assistant tells

- `caveat`, `worth noting`, `to be clear`, `let me be direct`, `here's the thing`,
  `that said`, `the thing is`
- `it's not X, it's Y`, `not just X but Y`
- `I hope this helps`, `great question`, `absolutely`, `certainly`

## Hedges

Say the thing, or do not say it.

- `you might want to consider possibly`
- `it seems like it could potentially`
- `arguably`, `somewhat`, `fairly`, `quite`, `basically`, `essentially`, `simply`, `just`

## Vague quantities

Give the number when you have it.

- `a lot of`, `several`, `various`, `numerous`, `a handful of`
- `significantly`, `dramatically`, `substantially`
